Why International Productions Choose Chile

01

Extreme Landscape Diversity

The world's driest desert (Atacama), Andean peaks, Pacific coastline, lakes, glaciers, vineyards, and cosmopolitan Santiago — all in one country. No other location offers this range within 2-3h flights.

02

35-45% Cost Savings vs USA

Crew rates run 35-45% of US IATSE union scale. Equipment rental 50-65% of US prices. Plus a 30% cash rebate on qualifying production spend (40% outside Santiago) through CORFO's IFI Audiovisual program.

03

Reversed Seasons

Need summer in December? Snow in July? Chile's Southern Hemisphere location means you can shoot "summer" content during the Northern Hemisphere winter — and vice versa. Year-round production capability.

04

Safety & Infrastructure

Highest safety rating in Latin America. Modern airports, highways, hotels and telecommunications. Reliable power grid even in remote locations. Santiago has direct flights from LAX, Miami, NYC, Dallas, Atlanta, Madrid, London, and more.

05

Experienced Local Crews

Chile has a mature film industry with crews experienced in international co-productions. Spanish and English-speaking professionals. Rental houses with ARRI, RED, and Sony Venice inventory.

06

Straightforward Permitting

Municipal permits in 5-15 business days. Film Commission Chile provides free location scouting support. Chile has co-production treaties with 12+ countries. We handle all paperwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about filming in Chile

A commercial production in Chile typically costs between $5,000 and $50,000 USD depending on crew size, shoot days, locations, and post-production. Chile crew costs run approximately 35-45% of US IATSE union rates, with 24% crew fringes (vs 30-40% in the US). Chile also offers a 30% cash rebate on qualifying production spend through the IFI Audiovisual program (40% outside Santiago).
Based on 2025 IATSE CPA scale adjusted for Chile: Director of Photography $500-$800/day, Camera Operator $280-$450, Gaffer $200-$350, Sound Mixer $280-$400, PA $80-$130. Chile uses a 10-hour base day with OT at 1.5x for hours 11-12, 2x after that, capped at 14 hours. Crew fringes are 24%.
Chile is 10-20% more expensive than Argentina/Colombia for labor, but offers: higher safety standards, superior infrastructure, extreme landscape diversity (Atacama desert to Patagonian glaciers), reversed seasons for year-round flexibility, reliable logistics, and the 30% production rebate. Many productions find the quality-to-cost ratio and reliability make Chile the better overall value.
Domestic flights from Santiago: Calama (Atacama) $150-$300 RT, Punta Arenas (Patagonia) $180-$350 RT, Temuco (Lakes District) $120-$250 RT. Flight times are 2-3 hours. Budget extra for ground transport from airports: 4x4 vehicles in Atacama ~$200-$400/day, vans in Patagonia ~$300-$600/day. Hotels range from $80-$250/night depending on remoteness.
